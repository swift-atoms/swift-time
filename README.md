# swift-time

Use the `Time` module when you need a wall-clock moment measured from the Unix epoch (`Time.Instant`), an exact amount of elapsed time in a named unit from `Time.Day` down to `Time.Yoctosecond` (any `Time.Unit`, converted with `Time.Conversion`), or a fixed UTC offset and the rule that picks one per instant (`Time.Zone`, `Time.Zone.Rule`). Use `Time Foundation Integration` only at the boundary with `Foundation.Date` and `Foundation.TimeZone`. To read or sleep on a monotonic clock, use swift-clock instead; to turn an instant into a calendar date and wall time, use swift-calendar, which builds on this package.

## Laws

- `Time.Instant` has nanosecond resolution and spans exactly the `Int64` range of seconds since the Unix epoch. The seconds are floored and the fraction always lies in `0..<1_000_000_000`, so one nanosecond before the epoch is second `-1` with fraction `999_999_999`.
- Advancing a `Time.Instant` never rounds. A displacement that is not a whole number of nanoseconds throws `.precision`, and leaving the `Int64` seconds range throws `.overflow`. The non-throwing forms (`+`, `-`, `Swift.InstantProtocol`, `Time.Epoch.instant(after:)` with a `Swift.Duration`) trap on the same conditions.
- `Time.Coordinate` is the attosecond-exact point behind `Time.Instant` and covers the whole `Swift.Duration` range. With the `Affine` trait, `Time.Coordinate.temporal` translates and measures without quantizing, and throws `.overflow` instead of wrapping.
- Every `Time.Unit` value is an exact rational number of that unit, and quantity conversion is exact in both directions with no bound on magnitude. `Time.Count<Unit>` and `Time.Offset<Unit>` hold fixed-width integers: their conversion throws `.inexact` when the result is not whole and `.overflow` when it does not fit. Every conversion rejects a zero or negative unit scale, even for a zero value.
- A `Time.Unit` encodes as `{"numerator": "…", "denominator": "…"}` with decimal strings, so values beyond 128 bits round-trip; zero encodes as `0` over `1`, and a zero denominator or a non-finite component fails to decode. A `Time.Instant` encodes as `secondsSinceUnixEpoch` and `nanosecondFraction`, and decoding a fraction outside its range fails.
- Days are a uniform 86,400 seconds. `Time.Conversion.components(fromSeconds:)` divides Euclidean-style, so negative seconds give a negative day count and a clock time inside the day. There are no leap seconds: `Time.Minute.Second` admits only `0...59`, so 23:59:60 is rejected rather than counted as the following midnight.
- A `Time.Zone` is a whole number of seconds. In `Time.Zone.hours(_:minutes:)` the minutes take the sign of the hours, so a negative offset under one hour is written with zero hours and negative minutes. Its description is `±HH:MM`, with `:SS` only when the seconds are non-zero.
- `Time.Zone.Rule.instant(local:ambiguous:skipped:)` reads `local` as wall time and finds the transition from the offsets 24 hours on either side, so it assumes at most one transition in that window. A repeated wall time resolves by the `ambiguous` `Time.Zone.Rule.Policy`; a skipped one moves by the gap, and the first skipped second resolved `.later` is the transition instant itself. Because of the probes, wall times within a day of the `Int64` bounds throw `.overflow`. The rule's closure runs on every query and nothing is cached.
- `Time.Instant.milliseconds(from:)` maps `nil` to `-1` (wait forever), truncates toward zero and saturates at the `CInt` bounds. `Swift.Duration.inSeconds` and its siblings are `Double` approximations that accept the full range.
- Converting a `Foundation.Date` to a `Time.Instant` floors the seconds and rounds the fraction to the nearest nanosecond; a non-finite or out-of-range date throws `.overflow`. The reverse goes through `Double`, so a round trip is exact only to about a microsecond. `Time.Zone.Rule` built from a `Foundation.TimeZone` reads `secondsFromGMT(for:)` at each instant.
